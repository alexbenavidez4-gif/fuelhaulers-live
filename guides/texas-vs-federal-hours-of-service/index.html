Texas vs. Federal Hours of Service Rules for Fuel Haulers

Updated 2026-11-23 · fuelhaulers.live

Fuel haulers in Texas run under one of two sets of hours of service rules. Which one you follow depends on whether your work is intrastate (staying in Texas) or interstate. Here is how they compare.

Which rules apply to you?

Drivers whose loads and routes stay inside Texas usually run on Texas intrastate rules, often called "Texas time." Drivers who cross state lines, or haul freight that is part of an interstate move, run on the federal rules from the FMCSA. Your carrier decides which rules you are dispatched under, so ask them if you are not sure.

Side by side

RuleTexas intrastateFederal (interstate)
Driving limit12 hours after 8 hours off in a row11 hours after 10 hours off in a row
Workday limitNo driving after 15 hours on dutyNo driving after the 14th hour after coming on duty
30-minute breakNot requiredRequired after 8 hours of driving. Any 30 minutes not driving counts, including waiting at the rack
Weekly limit70 hours on duty in 7 days60 hours in 7 days, or 70 hours in 8 days if the carrier runs every day
Restart34 hours off in a row34 hours off in a row
Short-haul (no logbook)Within 150 air miles, released within 12 hoursWithin 150 air miles, back within 14 hours

What counts as on-duty time?

Driving, plus everything you do for work that is not driving: pre-trip inspections, loading at the rack, waiting in line at the terminal, fueling, unloading at the drop, and paperwork. Off-duty and sleeper berth time do not count toward your weekly hours.

Bad weather and traffic

Under the federal rules, drivers who run into unexpected adverse driving conditions, such as bad weather or a road closure they could not have known about, can extend their driving time and workday by up to 2 hours.

Utah drivers

Utah generally follows the federal hours of service rules, including for drivers who stay inside Utah. Check with your carrier for any exceptions that apply to your operation.

Common questions

How many hours can you drive on Texas time?

Up to 12 hours of driving after 8 hours off in a row, with no driving after 15 hours on duty, and no more than 70 hours on duty in 7 days.

Do Texas intrastate drivers need a 30-minute break?

No. The 30-minute break is a federal rule. Texas intrastate rules do not require it.

Is there a 34-hour restart on Texas time?

Yes. Taking 34 or more hours off duty in a row restarts the 7-day period.
