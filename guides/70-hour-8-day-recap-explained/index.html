How the 70-Hour / 8-Day Recap Works (With Examples)

Updated 2026-11-23 · fuelhaulers.live

Your recap is how many on-duty hours you have used in the last 8 days, and how many you have left before you hit 70. Here is how to figure it, and how to know what you get back each night.

The rule

Under the federal rules, a driver whose carrier runs every day cannot drive after being on duty 70 hours in any 8 days in a row. If the carrier does not run every day, the limit is 60 hours in 7 days. The window rolls: each night at midnight the oldest day falls off and those hours come back.

A worked example

Say your last 8 days of on-duty time were:

DayOn duty
Day 1 (oldest)10 hrs
Day 212 hrs
Day 39 hrs
Day 411 hrs
Day 50 hrs (off)
Day 68 hrs
Day 710 hrs
Day 8 (today)7 hrs

Your total is 67 hours, so you have 3 hours left today. At midnight, Day 1's 10 hours drop off. Tomorrow you start with 70 minus 57, which is 13 hours available.

What to count

Count all on-duty time, not just driving: inspections, loading at the rack, waiting in line, fueling, unloading, and paperwork. Off-duty and sleeper berth time do not count.

Texas intrastate: 70 hours in 7 days

Drivers on Texas intrastate rules use 70 hours in 7 days instead of 8. The math is the same, but only the last 7 days count, so hours come back a day sooner.

Common questions

When do hours drop off a 70-hour recap?

At midnight. The oldest day in your 8-day window falls off, and those hours become available again.

Does waiting at the rack count toward my 70 hours?

Yes. Waiting at the terminal is on-duty not driving time, so it counts toward your 70 hours.
