The 34-Hour Restart: How It Works for Fuel Haulers

Updated 2026-11-23 · fuelhaulers.live

A 34-hour restart wipes your weekly hours back to zero. Here is how it works and when it is worth taking.

How it works

If you take 34 or more hours off duty in a row, your 7-day or 8-day period starts over. All the on-duty hours from before the restart stop counting, and you start the next shift with a full 60 or 70 hours available.

It is optional

You never have to take a restart. Without one, your hours simply roll off day by day at midnight. A restart is most useful when you are close to your limit and a lot of hours are stuck in the recent days of your window.

The old extra rules are not in effect

For a while, the federal restart had extra conditions: it had to include two 1 a.m. to 5 a.m. periods and could only be used once a week. Those conditions are not in effect. A restart today is simply 34 hours off in a row.

Texas intrastate

Texas intrastate rules also allow a 34-hour restart. Taking 34 or more hours off duty in a row restarts your 7-day period.

Tracking it

In the fuelhaulers.live HOS Recap calculator, turn on 34h Restart for the day you came back to work. The count starts over from that day.

Common questions

How long is a restart?

34 hours off duty in a row. Sleeper berth time counts as off duty for the restart.

Do I have to take a 34-hour restart?

No. It is optional. Without one, hours roll off your recap each night at midnight.
